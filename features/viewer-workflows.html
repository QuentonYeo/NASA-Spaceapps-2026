Viewer workflows

The home page offers two ways to start a SPHEREx session.

Validate a known object

Choose Vesta, Arosa, M101 or Barnard’s Star and select Start. Each example shows its field coordinates and why it is useful. The backend filters discovery by the example’s DR1 exposure IDs before scheduling cutouts. The viewer opens in Aligned Blink, selects the documented frames as they arrive and enables Gaia reference stars. Vesta and Arosa have amber trajectory overlays that can be toggled off. If the documented comparison is incomplete, the viewer shows available aligned frames and explains the fallback. If an archive request failed, select Start again to retry.

Vesta is selected by default and plays three verified D6 exposures in time order. Arosa demonstrates motion across two checked positions, M101 serves as a stationary alignment control, and Barnard’s Star is a subtle-motion exercise. The exact Barnard pair is a metadata-verified example with an unmeasured centroid shift. See the validation guide for dates, provenance and interpretation.

How astronomers check for motion describes the evidence to look for in either workflow.

Explore for movement

Enter RA, Dec and field of view, optionally choose a detector, or use a curated starting field. Suggestions explain why a field is useful for comparison. The viewer downloads the field in successive batches. When downloads finish, Start auto observation scans the retained FITS frames and ranks candidate tracks. Automatic motion search Selecting a detector restricts the search before image downloads. Changing fields still uses the existing FITS cutout cache where coverage allows.

Explore also includes the region spectrum. In native Browse observations, click or drag a sky aperture to load the 102-channel mosaic spectrum. This stationary-sky tool remains separate from Validate, which retains the guided blink, Gaia and Arosa controls. FOV controls stay first; the spectrum does not start from image navigation alone.

Automatic field ranking, a metadata-only timeline with arbitrary frame selection, and manual motion measurements are future work. The current suggestions describe known fields and do not indicate a detection.
