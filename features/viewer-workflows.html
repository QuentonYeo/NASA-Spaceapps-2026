Viewer workflows

The home page offers two ways to start a SPHEREx session. The known-object list shows each preset’s RA, Dec, and field of view in degrees. Choose a known object directly from the list to start its validation sequence, or enter coordinates in New observation and click Start to begin loading the field in Explore.

The home and observation views use a dark theme with native system typography, charcoal panels and blue controls. The home hero uses the Hubble image of M101.

Validate a known object

Choose Vesta, Arosa, M101 or Barnard’s Star and select Start. The active object’s name appears at the top of the validation view. Each preset shows its field coordinates and what to inspect. For these four curated examples, the backend filters discovery by DR1 exposure ID before scheduling cutouts. The viewer opens in Aligned Blink, selects the documented frames as they arrive and enables Gaia reference stars. Vesta and Arosa have amber trajectory overlays that can be toggled off. If the documented comparison is incomplete, the viewer shows available aligned frames and explains the fallback. If an archive request failed, select Start again to retry.

Epsilon Indi B (331.117°, −56.808°, 0.08°), WISE 0855−0714 (133.795129°, −7.245147°, 0.12°), RW Aurigae (76.956542°, +30.401417°, 0.06°), and R Draconis (248.167611°, +66.754979°, 0.06°) use archive field searches. They open in Aligned Blink with the available exposures and support manual frame selection. These presets have no verified exposure pair or target overlay. Compare the brown-dwarf fields across widely spaced dates. For the variable stars, compare brightness at the same position in similar-wavelength exposures with locked contrast. Barnard’s Star retains its existing curated exposures and field. RW Aurigae and R Draconis also offer Brightness over time from retained FITS exposures.

Auto observation appears immediately below the frame timeline in both workflows. Its button becomes available when loading has finished and there are retained FITS frames with aligned previews. It also works with preloaded sequences and can rerun a completed analysis.

A local demo can set VITE_PRELOADED_VALIDATION_SEQUENCES in frontend/.env.local to a JSON map from preset IDs to completed backend sequence IDs. Clicking a configured preset opens its saved frames. If the sequence expires, the viewer submits the preset search again.

Vesta is selected by default and plays three verified D6 exposures in time order. Arosa demonstrates motion across two checked positions, M101 serves as a stationary alignment control, and Barnard’s Star is a subtle-motion exercise. The exact Barnard pair is a metadata-verified example with an unmeasured centroid shift. See the validation guide for dates, provenance and interpretation.

How astronomers check for motion describes the evidence to look for in either workflow.

Observation view

The frames timeline and download progress sit above the enlarged image. Frame metadata sits below it. The timeline lists every available frame, even when blink playback uses a smaller set. Choosing an Auto observation candidate filters the timeline to its supporting frames. Select Show all frames to restore the field timeline. Auto observation sits below the timeline, and display settings and field controls sit below metadata. Explore’s native region and spectrum workflows remain available.

Explore for movement

Enter RA, Dec and field of view, optionally choose a detector, or use a curated starting field. Suggestions explain why a field is useful for comparison. The viewer downloads the field in successive batches. When downloads finish, Start auto observation scans the retained FITS frames and ranks candidate tracks. Automatic motion search Selecting a detector restricts the search before image downloads. Changing fields still uses the existing FITS cutout cache where coverage allows.

Explore also includes the region spectrum. In native Browse observations, click or drag a sky aperture to load the 102-channel mosaic spectrum. This stationary-sky tool remains separate from Validate, which retains the guided blink, Gaia and Arosa controls. FOV controls stay first; the spectrum does not start from image navigation alone.

When reviewing a moving candidate, use the link in its measurement panel to view the stationary-region spectrum. The spectrum panel links back to candidate analysis. On mobile, Image, Spectrum and Measurements tabs switch between region selection, the spectrum plot and its channel table; selecting or updating a sky region opens Spectrum.

Automatic field ranking, a metadata-only timeline with arbitrary frame selection, and manual motion measurements are future work. The current suggestions describe known fields and do not indicate a detection.
